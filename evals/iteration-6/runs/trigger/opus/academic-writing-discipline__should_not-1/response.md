Here's the corrected sentence:

**"The results show that perceived usefulness has a significant effect on intention."**

There were two subject–verb agreement errors:
1. **results shows → results show.** "Results" is plural, so the verb is "show."
2. **perceived usefulness have → perceived usefulness has.** "Perceived usefulness" is a single construct, so the verb is "has."

For an academic paper (e.g., TAM research), you could also consider:
- **"behavioral intention"** instead of just "intention," if that's the construct name in your model.
- **"significantly affects"** is a slightly shorter alternative: "The results show that perceived usefulness significantly affects behavioral intention."
- If your design is cross-sectional or correlational, "effect" can sound like a causal claim. A more cautious option is "is significantly and positively associated with" or "significantly predicts."
